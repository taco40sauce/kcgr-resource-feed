Winlink & PAT — Emergency Digital Messaging
Jump to: ① Get Started ② Pick Your Software ③ First Message ④ File a KCGR Report ⑤ Regional Nets ⑥ National Nets ⑦ Annual Exercises ⑧ PAT Notes ⑨ Field Tips
When normal communications fail, Winlink keeps working. It moves standard ICS forms, welfare messages, and situation reports over RF — no internet required at the field end. This guide gets you from zero to your first check-in, then gives you a directory of recurring nets and exercises to build real readiness. Both Winlink Express (Windows) and PAT (Linux, Mac, Windows) are treated as first-class options throughout.

Winlink is a worldwide radio messaging system that moves email-style messages and ICS forms over amateur radio frequencies — without requiring the internet at the sending station. A network of gateway stations (RMS nodes) bridges your RF message to the Winlink servers, which forward it to its destination.

In an emergency, this means a net control station, an EOC, or a served agency can receive structured ICS traffic from field operators even when cell service and the internet are down — as long as someone on each end has a radio and the right software.

What you need to get started

  • A valid amateur radio license (any class — Technician gives you VHF/UHF access)
  • A radio capable of FM or HF operation
  • A sound-card interface (SignaLink, Digirig, or similar) or a radio with built-in USB audio (IC-7100, IC-7300, many Yaesu mobiles)
  • Winlink Express or PAT installed on your computer
  • A free Winlink account — register at winlink.org
Telnet first. Both Winlink Express and PAT support Telnet mode — messages route over the internet, not RF. It is not a substitute for RF readiness, but it is the fastest way to verify your account, learn the software, and send your first message without needing a sound-card interface.
AA4TE field note: The IC-7100's built-in USB audio and CI-V control make it one of the cleaner setups for Winlink on Linux. No separate sound-card interface, no driver headaches — PAT sees it as a standard audio device. Six years in, still the radio I reach for first.

Two mature clients handle the vast majority of Winlink traffic. They use the same network and the same account — your callsign works in either one.

Not sure which to start with? If you run Windows and your ARES group uses Winlink Express, start there — fewer compatibility questions during exercises. If you run Linux or Mac, PAT is the right choice and handles the overwhelming majority of nets and exercises without issue.

The fastest path to a first successful message is Telnet — internet-connected, no RF hardware required. Once you have that working, move to VARA FM or Packet over RF.

Step 1 — Register your callsign

Go to winlink.org/user and create a free account using your callsign as the username. Password recovery requires a working email address — use one you check.

Step 2 — Install your client

Winlink Express: Download from winlink.org, run the installer, enter your callsign and password on first launch.

PAT: Download the latest release from github.com/la5nta/pat/releases for your platform. On Linux, extract and run pat configure to set your callsign, password, and locator.

Step 3 — Send a Telnet check-in

In Winlink Express: Open Message → New Message. Address it to WINLINK. Subject: Test. Body: your callsign and grid square. Send via Telnet.

In PAT: Open the browser UI at http://localhost:8080. Compose → To: WINLINK. Connect via Telnet. Watch the transfer log — a successful session shows sent and received counts.

Step 4 — Verify delivery

Log in to winlink.org and check your account's message history. A sent message that shows up there means your credentials and network path are working correctly.

Step 5 — Move to RF

Find a local RMS gateway using the Winlink channel list or the map at winlink.org. Program the frequency into your radio, configure your sound-card interface or USB audio, and attempt a VARA FM or Packet session. This is where the real readiness lives — Telnet is practice, RF is the mission.

AA4TE field note: Don't wait for an activation to discover your gateway is down or your audio levels are wrong. Run a weekly RF check-in — Original Winlink Wednesday or SC Winlink are both good habits. The first time you need it in the field should not be the first time it works.
Ready to put this to work? If you'd rather practice first without touching the public map, use one of the practice nets in Field Tips — Original Winlink Wednesday is a good weekly habit and doesn't go anywhere near KCGR-OPS. If you're ready for the real thing, skip straight to Filing a KCGR Report below — a correctly addressed message to KCGR-OPS posts to the live map, no test flag needed.
The first time Winlink works over RF in the field, you'll understand why you practiced.

This section is the local, specific part: filing a real Kershaw County Ground Report (KCGR-RS) over Winlink, addressed to the tactical station KCGR-OPS. Everything here works the same whether you're on Telnet or RF — KCGR-OPS doesn't care how the message reached it.

Which path do I need?
  • Have the KCGR form set installed already? → Filing with the form, below.
  • First time, in a hurry, or never installed the form? → The no-form / cut-paste path — just as valid, nothing about it is second-best. See the note at the end of this section on why.

Both paths work in both Winlink Express and PAT — pick your path first, then find your software inside it.

The Form Path

The KCGR Resource Status form is a real Winlink custom form set — it fills in the same message a no-form report would, just with dropdowns and validation instead of typing labels by hand. It supports up to 3 observations per message.

Winlink Express

Step 1 — Install the form

Download all the files from the KCGR repo's winlink_forms folder and copy them into:

C:\RMS Express\Global Folders\Templates\KCGR\

Step 2 — Start a new message

Message → New Message → Select Template, then pick KCGR Resource Status from the KCGR folder.

Step 3 — Fill it out and send

Address it to KCGR-OPS yourself — the form's own "Send To" field is a reminder, not the real address. Fill in what you actually observed (see field guide below), leave the rest blank, and send.

PAT

Step 1 — Install the form

Download all the files from the KCGR repo's winlink_forms folder and copy them into:

~/.local/share/pat/Standard_Forms/kcgr_forms/

(Create the kcgr_forms folder if it doesn't already exist.)

Step 2 — Start a new message

Action → Compose..., then Template… in the compose window.

Open the kcgr_forms folder and select KCGR_ResourceStatus.

Step 3 — Fill it out

The form opens in your browser, pre-filled with your callsign and the current time. Fill in what you observed, leave the rest blank.

Step 4 — Submit and send

Click Submit at the bottom of the form. It closes the browser tab and drops the filled-in report into PAT's compose window as the message body. Check that To: shows KCGR-OPS — add it yourself if the field is empty — then click Post to send.

AA4TE field note: Here's Report 1 filled out for a real scenario — the Elgin BP on Hwy 1 has fuel, pumps running, but there's a line. Category, Status, and a 2-letter Specifier are the fields that matter most; Notes is free text.

Category codes: KCGR-SHLT (shelter) · KCGR-MED (medical) · KCGR-ROAD (road/bridge) · KCGR-H2O (potable water) · KCGR-FOOD (food/grocery) · KCGR-FUEL (fuel) · KCGR-PWR (power/charging) · KCGR-CELL (cell signal) · KCGR-ATM (financial access) · KCGR-HW (health & welfare relay)

Status codes: OP (open/operational) · CL (closed) · LM (limited) · CA (caution — accessible but hazardous) · UK (unknown/not directly observed)

Specifier is a 2-letter code specific to your category — keep your KCGR Quick Reference card handy for that list rather than memorizing it here.

The No-Form / Cut-Paste Path

No form installed? No problem — the parser reads the message by its section headers and field labels, not by which software sent it. Get those two things typed exactly right and it works identically to a message sent from the real form.

Winlink Express

Step 1 — New plain message

Message → New Message. Do not select a template.

Step 2 — Address it

To: KCGR-OPS. Subject must contain KCGR-RS — e.g. KCGR-RS - YOURCALL - 2026-09-26.

Step 3 — Paste and fill

Copy the template below into the message body, fill in the blanks, send.

PAT

Step 1 — New plain message

Action → Compose... Skip the Template… button entirely.

Step 2 — Address it and paste

To: KCGR-OPS. Subject must contain KCGR-RS. Paste the template below into the body and fill in the blanks.

Step 3 — Send it

Click Post in the compose window toolbar.

Copy this block and fill in the blanks:

Worked example — the same Elgin BP fuel report from above, written by hand:

KERSHAW COUNTY GROUND REPORT -- RESOURCE STATUS (KCGR-RS)
==========================================================

--- REPORTER INFO ---
Reporter Callsign: AA4TE

Date: 16
Time: 1430

Latitude: 34.171358
Longitude: -80.790513

Reporting more than one thing? Copy a second --- REPORT 2 --- block underneath (same field labels), up to REPORT 3. Skip blocks you're not using — no empty placeholders needed.

AA4TE field note — if you get this wrong, it still makes it to an operator via email. Missing something in this format won't make a report vanish silently:
  • Getting everything correct puts the information on the map automatically.
  • Leave Category blank, and that report block is just skipped — not an error, as if it wasn't sent.
  • Fill in a Category that isn't a real code, and it still publishes — flagged for a human to clean up, never dropped.
  • Leave Status blank or get it wrong, and it auto-publishes as UK - unknown instead of being rejected.
  • Everything else — Specifier, Location, Date, Time, Notes, coordinates — is optional. Blank is fine.
  • Latitude and Longitude — is optional. However, it's preferred so an operator can more easily pinpoint the location on the map.'.

This isn't a loophole in the system — it's the design. KCGR-RS is built for the operator who's never been trained on the format, not just the one who has. An imperfect report that gets through beats a perfect one that never gets sent.

📡 PAT on Linux & Mac
Modern PAT is more capable than a good deal of older internet advice suggests. It supports VARA, ARDOP, AX.25, PACTOR, Telnet, position reporting, simultaneous P2P listening, a forms/templates engine, form catalog, attachments, and a text-template editor. The advice to "just use Winlink Express" often dates from 2018–2020 and doesn't reflect current PAT capability.

Works cleanly in PAT

  • HAMWORD — plain text message, immediate response
  • Standard Original Winlink Wednesday check-ins
  • GLAWN plain-text check-ins
  • East Coast simple messages
  • W0NE standard check-ins
  • North Texas — use the published tab-delimited format
  • Any exercise requesting ordinary text or an ordinary attachment
  • Iowa ARES Winlink Weekend — Telnet explicitly accepted
  • ShakeOut and Tsunami exercises — standard Winlink message format

Use with caution — test before the deadline

ETO Winlink Thursdays explicitly warns that alternative-client users are "on their own" for certain import-and-reply exercises. Winlink Express is recommended for those specific assignments. Standard ICS-213 and ICS-205 submission works fine in PAT.
KE7ADD Weekly WinLink publishes custom-form installation instructions for Winlink Express and RadioMail. The pipe-delimited text record is shown on the net page — contact Chip, KE7ADD directly to confirm whether a manually constructed PAT message receives full scoring credit before your first submission.
Standard ICS forms in PAT generally work, but a locally modified form or an exercise requiring an exact XML attachment should be tested before the deadline. PAT's form catalog has grown substantially — check current release notes before assuming a form isn't available.

Telnet policy varies

Some nets accept Telnet specifically to help beginners. Original Winlink Wednesday currently wants the message to leave your station by RF. Check the current week's net notice — don't assume last month's policy still applies.

AA4TE on the IC-7100 + PAT: The combination works well on Linux. USB audio and CI-V control both route through the same cable. PAT sees the radio as a standard audio device — no separate SignaLink, no FTDI driver issues. VARA FM through the IC-7100's built-in soundcard is stable and fast. If you're setting this up for the first time, start with PAT's Telnet mode, then add VARA FM once the account and software are confirmed working.

Telnet is the right place to learn the software, verify your account, and understand how Winlink messaging works. It is not emergency communications practice. When the internet is down, Telnet is down. Build your RF path — VARA FM, Packet, or HF — and exercise it regularly. Your local RMS gateway, your audio levels, and your antenna are the things that matter in an activation.

Most operators use one RMS gateway and have never tried another. Gateways go offline — hardware fails, operators relocate, power goes out. Know at least two gateways you can reach from your normal operating position, on different bands if possible. The Brunswick County EmComm exercises include a specific drill on finding an alternative gateway when your normal one fails. It's worth practicing.

ICS-213 is the workhorse, but your county ARES or served agency may require ICS-214, ICS-205, or a custom form. Find out which forms matter in your jurisdiction and practice them under the ETO Thursdays rotation — ETO covers ICS-213, ICS-213 Resource Request, ICS-205, and Field Situation Reports in a four-week cycle. Field-by-field feedback from ETO will show you errors you didn't know you were making.

The Florida Winlink Net tracks power source in check-in reports — battery, generator, or solar. That field exists because power source matters in emergency ops. Run at least one Winlink session per month from battery power. Know how long your battery lasts at your normal transmit duty cycle. If you run solar, know what happens when the cloud cover drops your panel output mid-session.

If you're in South Carolina and building a Winlink practice routine from scratch, this order works well:

  1. Original Winlink Wednesday over RF — baseline weekly RF readiness.
  2. HAMWORD — easy PAT test, quick feedback, keeps the RF path exercised between bigger nets.
  3. KE7ADD Weekly WinLink — rewards APRSLink, different modes, portable operation, and emergency power. Aligns well with KCGR-type deployments.
  4. ETO Winlink Thursdays — structured form accuracy with feedback. Use Winlink Express for the import-and-reply exercises if you run PAT.
  5. October ShakeOut — realistic ground-report practice, international participation, direct parallel with KCGR field operations.
